The third sibling, after Ariane and Maison

Hora, the night watch.

Night watchmen used to walk the town and call the hours: "Ten o'clock, and all is well." Hora is that watcher, as a little owl. It dozes while everything runs, opens one eye when something is slow, and wakes only for a real outage. Flapping never wakes it, so it never wakes you.

01

Who keeps watch

Hora means "hours". The Horai kept the hours and the gates of Olympus. The night watch kept the hours of a town and said, every hour, that all was well. A status page does exactly that.

Why an owl

The animal of the night watch, and Athena's little owl (Athene noctua), Greek like the Horai. Its tufts are Maison's ears, its round body is Ariane's ball of yarn: the same family drawing.

Why asleep

The promise is "flapping never wakes you up". An owl dozing in front of an all-green page says it better than any badge: calm is the normal state, and a closed eye is earned by retries, quorum and root-cause grouping.

Why several

One owl can mishear. Hora nodes watch from several places and ask each other before they hoot: an outage is called when two agree. A group of owls is a parliament; this one votes before it wakes you.

Why cute, and still serious

The owl lives in one place per page, at the size of a sentence's companion. Everything else is plain: Fraunces, Cal Sans, tabular numbers, words for every state. Cute in the corner, precise in the table.

"Saturday 14:32. Everything is running."The hero of every status page is the watchman's call: the hour, then the state, in one sentence.

All well. Three places, three owls asleep.

Local only. Paris wakes, asks, the others see it up: a notice, not a page.

Confirmed. All three see it down: one alert, with the cause.

02

The owl and its states

The pose is the overall state of the page. The badge repeats the state's shape, and the words beside it always say the same thing.

All up

Asleep. Nothing is wrong, so nothing moves.

Slow

Down

Awake, tufts up. Attentive, never alarmed. Badge: diamond.

assets/owl-down.svg

Maintenance

A planned nap, tufts folded. Badge: clock.

assets/owl-maint.svg

No data

Two dashes, no blush; it cannot see yet. Badge: dashed ring.

assets/owl-none.svg
Never sad, never scared

No mouth, no tears, no frown. Down is "awake and attentive". It shows what Hora is doing, not how it feels about you.

Moves only in answer

No idle loop. It moves once when the state changes (the tufts perk on waking), then stays still.

Never alone

Always next to the sentence that says the state, with the state's shape in its badge. The owl is decoration for anyone who can't see it.

Reduced motion

Poses stay; transitions and the perk are dropped. A screen reader hears the sentence, not "owl".

One per page

In the hero, the door or an empty state. Never in rows, tables or charts. The mesh view is the one place where several perch together.

Can be turned off

Operators who want a neutral page set [page] mascot = false (proposal): the sentence and the shapes carry everything anyway.

08

What the niche taught us

From Atlassian Statuspage, Instatus, Better Stack, OpenStatus, Hyperping, incident.io, Uptime Kuma, Gatus and Cachet (from knowledge of these products; not re-checked online for this pass).

Kept

  • One headline sentence for the whole page (Statuspage, incident.io).
  • 90-day bars with uptime % per service, 30 days on phones (Statuspage).
  • Incident updates as a short, dated, newest-first timeline (Statuspage, Instatus).
  • Follow updates near the headline (feed, JSON, badges).
  • "What you'll notice" impact wording (incident.io, Better Stack).

Avoided

  • Colour-only state: green/red dots, bars with no shape (most of them).
  • Operator jargon on public pages: p95, SLO, "heartbeating" (Uptime Kuma, Gatus).
  • Noisy grids: hundreds of equal cards, every bar drawn as 90 nodes.
  • Hover-only details that touch and keyboard users never reach.
  • Full-page refresh that loses scroll and focus.

Hora's own

  • Only what is wrong, up front: "Needs attention", then groups folded with a summary.
  • Shapes in the bar: shorter is slow, a drop below the line is down.
  • Checked from several places, and a calm public sentence when they disagree.
  • The watchman's call: the hour, then the state.
  • A face, with rules: the owl never carries the state alone.
09

Mockups

Plain HTML and CSS, light and dark (system, or the toggle), checked at 320, 360, 1440 and 2560 px without sideways scroll. Thumbnails: desktop light, phone dark.

All up status-ok.html

The hero is the watchman's call. Groups as columns of framed rows; Rows or Cards (cards add per-place response times and a one-path chart). "Who keeps watch" closes the page.

Outage, slow service, announcement status-incident.html

Brick wash, awake owl, one sentence with the time. "Right now" puts the story first: what you'll notice, confirmed from 3 places, dated updates.

Local only: not an outage status-local.htmladded for the mesh

Paris can't reach the API, Frankfurt and Montréal can. Visitors read "Everything is running" and one calm note; operators open "Why nobody was woken".

Planned maintenance status-maintenance.html

The owl naps, the clock shape marks what is paused, a progress bar shows the window. The next maintenance is announced below.

712 monitors status-scale.html

What is wrong first, then 7 groups folded with counts and one group bar each. The open group lists 24 compact rows, paged. 16 KB of content.

A monitor's history monitor.html

Plain-language tiles, a chart with HTML labels, 90 focusable days with tooltips that work on touch, an hour-by-hour week, each place's view; percentiles behind "For operators".

Post-mortem incident.html

Title says what happened and how long. Timeline with state shapes, "seen down from 3 of 3", the captured answer, Copy as Markdown.

Watchers (operators) watchers.htmladded for the mesh

The mesh: heartbeats, a link split reported as a split, confirmations over 30 days, each service from each place.

Monthly report report.html

An A4 sheet on screen, print-ready: five headline numbers, a table per group with the target met or missed in words, how it is counted.

Empty and private empty.html

First run with a copyable snippet, the door for a private page (Maison's AuthShell), a monitor waiting for its first check.

A docs page docs-page.htmladded for the docs

Every docs page wears the brand: wordmark, search, sidebar with the current page, "On this page", Fraunces headings, TOML and shell blocks with Copy, a table, and the four asides on the state system. Real content: the peers guide.

Docs and README docs-home.html

Starlight splash re-themed: tagline, install, the owl on the dial of hours, the blip-to-alert flow, a comparison table and the README header.
